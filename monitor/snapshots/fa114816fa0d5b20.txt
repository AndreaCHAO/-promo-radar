The Rise of 24/7 Trading: Opportunities and Challenges for CFD Brokers
The Rise of 24/7 Trading: Opportunities and Challenges for CFD Brokers
2026-11-10T15:00:00.000+02:00
T u e s d a y , 1 0 / 1 1 / 2 0 2 6 | 1 3 : 0 0 G M T by
Sylwester Majewski
Major retail brokers are expanding into synthetic weekend CFDs on gold, oil, and indices, but internalizing client flow without underlying cash markets concentrates dangerous unhedged risk onto dealing desks.
Offering continuous access helps firms defend trading volumes against crypto venues, yet wider spreads, thin pricing feeds, and Monday gap risk could severely punish liquidity providers and client retention.
Products
The
traditional five-day trading week is coming under increasing pressure.
Throughout 2026, a growing number of FX/CFD brokers have introduced products
that allow clients to trade seven days a week, including weekends when most
underlying financial markets remain closed.
Gold has
become the main focus of this expansion, with Vantage, VT Markets, XM,
STARTRADER, CFI Financial Group and CMC Markets among the brokers introducing
weekend trading. Others, including FxPro and easyMarkets, have extended the
offering to oil, silver and selected equity indices.
However,
keeping a trading platform open is relatively straightforward compared with the
more complicated task of providing reliable prices, sufficient liquidity and
effective risk management throughout the weekend.
London's trading industry is coming home!
Benefits
and Risks of 24/7 Trading
For retail
traders, the main attraction is flexibility. Financial markets are increasingly
influenced by events that do not follow traditional trading hours. Geopolitical
developments, elections, military conflicts and unexpected policy announcements
can occur on Saturdays and Sundays, leaving investors unable to respond through
conventional instruments until markets reopen.
Weekend
CFDs allow traders to react immediately, potentially hedge existing positions
or speculate on how prices will respond when regular trading resumes.
Meanwhile,
continuous trading creates for brokers an opportunity to increase client
activity, attract new customers and compete with cryptocurrency exchanges and
prediction markets that already operate throughout the weekend. Liquidity
providers also see a commercial opportunity, as brokers need new pricing feeds
and execution services to support these products.
The trade-off between continuous access and market infrastructure
Potential benefit Corresponding challenge
Immediate response to weekend events Limited underlying-market liquidity
More opportunities for client trading Wider spreads and execution costs
Weekend hedging opportunities Limited availability of external hedges
Continuous price discovery Synthetic pricing and basis risk
Additional revenue opportunities Higher operational and risk-management costs
Greater competition with crypto platforms Continuous monitoring and collateral requirements
Source: Finance Magnates Intelligence (based on FIA and CFTC)
In its
March 2026 roundtable report, the Futures Industry Association (FIA)
highlighted growing demand from both retail and institutional investors.
Coinbase's Liz Martin noted that its futures exchange regularly experiences
billion-dollar weekend trading days, while Marex's Thomas Texier pointed to
increasing interest from asset managers and corporations seeking to manage risk
before Monday's opening.
However,
greater accessibility does not necessarily mean better trading conditions.
Weekend liquidity may be considerably thinner than during conventional
sessions, resulting in wider spreads, greater price volatility and potentially
more expensive execution.
There is
also a fundamental difference between trading an instrument on an established
exchange and trading a synthetic CFD whose price is determined by a broker or
liquidity provider. In the latter case, the trader may be exposed to prices
that do not fully reflect those available when the underlying market reopens.

Pricing and
Hedging: Key Weekend Trading Challenges
One of the
biggest questions surrounding 24/7 CFDs is what happens when a trader opens a
position while the underlying market is closed. During conventional trading
hours, a liquidity provider can use spot markets, futures or other derivatives
to hedge its exposure. However, when major exchanges close for the weekend,
those options become limited. The broker or liquidity provider must either find
an alternative market, offset opposing client positions or retain some of the
exposure until conventional trading resumes.
Learn more
about the challenges of pricing and hedging, and how the industry is addressing
them, in our full analysis on the Finance Magnates Intelligence portal.
